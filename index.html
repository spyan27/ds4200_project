“OpenRx”: Making Medicare Patient-Administered Drug Pricing More Transparent 2019-2023

Overall Introduction

This project explores how the prices of patient-administered drugs covered under Medicare Part D changed from 2019 to 2023. In the first part, we want to investigate the changes through the Covid-19, manufacturer-level shifts, and evolving health policy landscapes. Our goal is to make drug pricing trends more transparent by identifying price hikes, mapping policy effects, and visualizing how costs vary by manufacturer. In the second part, we put these prices in context of the users of Medicare, mainly elderly age 65+. We identify the top 5 chronic conditions affecting this group, and group drug pricing trends by diseases they treat. This website features both accessible dashboards and static visuals that help both policy makers and patients understand where spending is growing and why. (can add more details later)

To learn more about the Medicare Drug D data, go to rate and its implications, you can visit the Medicare.gov website.

Overview of the Pricing Trends

First, we present an overview of the average drug price from 2019 to 2023. The average price shows a steady increase in average Part D drug prices from 2019 to 2023, with average prices rising from approximately $305 to $563 over this period. We hypothesize that this increase may be attributed to several factors, including inflationary pressures, supply chain disruptions, and changes in healthcare policies during the COVID-19 pandemic. We will explore that further in later sections.

Overview of the Pricing Trends

Using the dropdown menu, we can look at each of the five chronic conditions individually and compare their total claims and average spending per claim from 2019 to 2023. Looking at the visualization, we can see that for hypertension, the total number of claims is the highest every year and increases slightly from around 253 million claims in 2019 to around 270 million claims in 2023, a growth of roughly 6.7%. However, the average spending per claim rises much more sharply, from around $1,501 in 2019 to about $2,003 in 2023, increasing roughly 33%. This suggests that while the overall use of hypertension medications remained relatively stable with a slight increase, the cost per prescription fill has grown significantly over time.

The relationship between unemployment rate and other social factors

Moreover, we also want to examine the relationship between unemployment rate and other social factors in the data. All features are numerical thus we firts create a correlation plot to examine whether there are linear relationship between unemployment rate with other social factors. Notice that if the correlation is small, it only means that there is no potential linear relationship between two features.

Based on the correlation plot, among all the features, poverty has the strongest correlation with the unemployment rate since they are both related to the economics. The percentage of single parents is the second important feature which inidicates the stability of family is also strongly related to the job market. The increase of unemployment rate may also related to the increase of violent crime and murder rate but has a negative relationship with education indicators, like the percentage of people who go to high school and college. The only variable that has no direct linear relation with the unemployment rate is Metropolitan, which means the percent of residents living in a metropolitan area.

We have also create an interactive scatter plot so that our users can select a couple of states within a certain range of unemployment rate and to see how the other factors may be related here.
