“OpenRx”: Making Medicare Patient-Administered Drug Pricing More Transparent 2019-2023

Overall Introduction

This project explores how the prices of patient-administered drugs covered under Medicare Part D changed from 2019 to 2023. Medicare Part D is defined as prescription drug coverage for Medicare enrollees. Original Medicare (Parts A & B) does NOT cover prescription medications, so Part D fills that gap. In the first part, we want to investigate general pricing changes during this period. Our goal is to make drug pricing trends more transparent by identifying price hikes, mapping policy effects, and visualizing how costs vary by manufacturer. In the second part, we put these prices in context of the users of Medicare, mainly elderly age 65+. We identify the top 5 chronic conditions affecting this group, and group drug pricing trends by diseases they treat. This website features both accessible dashboards and static visuals that help both policy makers and patients understand where spending is growing and why.

To learn more about the Medicare Drug D data, you can visit the Medicare.gov website and explore their Guide to Medicare Drug Coverage for rates and implications and how to enroll.

Introduction to the data

Our analysis makes use of one main dataset called medicare_drug_spending.csv, which contains the following Medicare Drug D pricing data from 2019-2023. The original dataset’s full data dictionary can be found here. Key information about the features used in our analysis is listed in the following table:

Variable Name Details
Brnd_Name Brand Name – The trademarked name of the drug filled.
Gnrc_Name Generic Name – The chemical ingredient of the drug rather than the trademarked brand name.
Tot_Mftr Number of Manufacturers – Total number of manufacturers producing the drug.
Tot_Spndng_[Year] Total Spending – Aggregate Medicare Part D spending for the drug in a given benefit year.
Tot_Benes_[Year] Percent of residents with a high school degree
Avg_Spnd_Per_Dsg_Unt_Wghtd_[Year] Average Spending per Dosage Unit (Weighted) – Total Part D drug spending divided by total dosage units, weighted by proportion of total claims.

Outlier_Flag_[Year] Outlier Flag – “1” when a drug’s average spending per dosage unit is substantially impacted by outlier records in that year.

Data Cleaning and Preparation

To create better data visualizations, we performed several cleaning steps on this dataset. First, we restructured the data by converting it from wide to long format, creating separate rows for each year's pricing data. This transformation allows us to analyze year-over-year price changes more effectively. Next, we identified and removed outlier prices using the Outlier_Flag variable provided in the dataset, which resulted in more representative average drug prices.

Focus on Chronic Conditions

To study the population that Medicare primarily serves—elderly adults age 65+ and people with disabilities—we focused on the top 5 chronic conditions that affect elderly adults most based on the National Council on Aging’s article. These conditions are hypertension(61%), high cholesterol(55%), arthritis(51%), obesity(40%), and diabetes(24%). We used the ATC (Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical) classification system, developed by the World Health Organization, to identify relevant medications. This system organizes drugs based on the body system they target and their therapeutic purpose. Using the WHO ATC Index , we selected drug classes associated with each of the five chronic conditions. We then developed a Python script (build_atc_mapping.py) to systematically match ATC disease prefixes to generic drug names in our dataset. For example, drugs with ATC codes beginning with "C09" (agents acting on the renin-angiotensin system) were mapped to hypertension, while "A10" codes (drugs used in diabetes) were mapped to diabetes medications. All subsequent analyses are based on ATC coding of reported medications.

Overview of the Pricing Trends

First, we present an overview of the average drug price from 2019 to 2023. The average price shows a steady increase in average Part D drug prices from 2019 to 2023, with average prices rising from approximately $273 to $537 over this period. There is a steeper rise of the average price from 2021 to 2022, from $244 to $464, representing a nearly 90% increase in just one year. We hypothesize that this increase may be attributed to several factors, including inflationary pressures, supply chain disruptions, and changes in healthcare policies during the COVID-19 pandemic.

The relationship between unemployment rate and other social factors

Moreover, we also want to examine the relationship between unemployment rate and other social factors in the data. All features are numerical thus we firts create a correlation plot to examine whether there are linear relationship between unemployment rate with other social factors. Notice that if the correlation is small, it only means that there is no potential linear relationship between two features.

Based on the correlation plot, among all the features, poverty has the strongest correlation with the unemployment rate since they are both related to the economics. The percentage of single parents is the second important feature which inidicates the stability of family is also strongly related to the job market. The increase of unemployment rate may also related to the increase of violent crime and murder rate but has a negative relationship with education indicators, like the percentage of people who go to high school and college. The only variable that has no direct linear relation with the unemployment rate is Metropolitan, which means the percent of residents living in a metropolitan area.

We have also create an interactive scatter plot so that our users can select a couple of states within a certain range of unemployment rate and to see how the other factors may be related here.
