“OpenRx”: Making Medicare Patient-Administered Drug Pricing More Transparent 2019-2023

Overall Introduction

This project explores how the prices of patient-administered drugs covered under Medicare Part D changed from 2019 to 2023. Medicare Part D is defined as prescription drug coverage for Medicare enrollees. Original Medicare (Parts A & B) does NOT cover prescription medications, so Part D fills that gap. In the first part, we want to investigate general pricing changes during this period. In the second part, we analyze pricing trends and how they vary by manufacturer. In the final part, we put these prices in context of the users of Medicare, mainly elderly age 65+. We identify the top 5 chronic conditions affecting this group, and group drug pricing trends by diseases they treat. Our goal is to make drug pricing trends more transparent by identifying price hikes and and visualizing how costs vary by manufacturer and by diseases. This website features both accessible dashboards and static visuals that help both policy makers and patients understand where spending is growing and why.

To learn more about the Medicare Drug D data, you can visit the Medicare.gov website and explore their Guide to Medicare Drug Coverage for rates and implications and how to enroll. To learn more about top diseases affecting elderly adults, you can visit the National Council on Aging website. To access the WHO ATC Index, you can visit the WHO ATC Index.

Introduction to the data

Our analysis makes use of one main dataset called medicare_drug_spending.csv, which contains the following Medicare Drug D pricing data from 2019-2023. The original dataset’s full data dictionary can be found here. Key information about the features used in our analysis is listed in the following table:

Variable Name Details
Brnd_Name Brand Name – The trademarked name of the drug filled.
Gnrc_Name Generic Name – The chemical ingredient of the drug rather than the trademarked brand name.
Tot_Mftr Number of Manufacturers – Total number of manufacturers producing the drug.
Tot_Spndng_[Year] Total Spending – Aggregate Medicare Part D spending for the drug in a given benefit year.
Tot_Benes_[Year] Percent of residents with a high school degree
Avg_Spnd_Per_Dsg_Unt_Wghtd_[Year] Average Spending per Dosage Unit (Weighted) – Total Part D drug spending divided by total dosage units, weighted by proportion of total claims.
Avg_Spnd_Per_Clm_[Year] Average Spending per Claim – Total drug spending divided by the number of prescription claims.
Outlier_Flag_[Year] Outlier Flag – “1” when a drug’s average spending per dosage unit is substantially impacted by outlier records in that year.

Data Cleaning and Preparation

To create better data visualizations, we performed several cleaning steps on this dataset. First, we restructured the data by converting it from wide to long format, creating separate rows for each year's pricing data. This transformation allows us to analyze year-over-year price changes more effectively. Next, we identified and removed outlier prices using the Outlier_Flag variable provided in the dataset, which resulted in more representative average drug prices. Additionally, we filtered the data to include only rows where Mftr_Name was 'Overall', as these rows aggregate prices across all manufacturers for each generic drug. This approach prevents double-counting prices when a drug has multiple manufacturers.

To study the population that Medicare primarily serves—elderly adults age 65+ and people with disabilities—we focused on the top 5 chronic conditions that affect elderly adults most based on the National Council on Aging’s article. These conditions are hypertension(61%), high cholesterol(55%), arthritis(51%), obesity(40%), and diabetes(24%). We used the ATC (Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical) classification system, developed by the World Health Organization, to identify relevant medications. This system organizes drugs based on the body system they target and their therapeutic purpose. Using the WHO ATC Index, we selected drug classes associated with each of the five chronic conditions. We then developed a Python script (build_atc_mapping.py) to systematically match ATC disease prefixes to generic drug names in our dataset. For example, drugs with ATC codes beginning with "C09" (agents acting on the renin-angiotensin system) were mapped to hypertension, while "A10" codes (drugs used in diabetes) were mapped to diabetes medications. All subsequent analyses are based on ATC coding of reported medications.

Overview of the Pricing Trends

First, we present an overview of the average drug price from 2019 to 2023. The average price shows a steady increase in average Part D drug prices from 2019 to 2023, with average prices rising from approximately $273 to $537 over this period. There is a steeper rise of the average price from 2021 to 2022, from $244 to $464, representing a nearly 90% increase in just one year. We hypothesize that this increase may be attributed to several factors, including inflationary pressures, supply chain disruptions, and changes in healthcare policies during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Pricing Trends by Manufacturer

To better understand Medicare drug pricing trends, we examined the spending patterns of pharmaceutical manufacturers directly.

Comparing generic versus brand drugs, total spending on brand drugs is consistently much higher than on generics. This difference reflects the fact that branded drugs are typically protected by patents, giving manufacturers the ability and incentive to set higher prices. Our analysis also highlights that spending for both brand and generic drugs increases steadily over time, with the relative proportion between them remaining fairly consistent.

The treemap highlights the concentration of Medicare drug spending among a few dominant pharmaceutical manufacturers. While users can interact with the visualization to filter by disease category, the overall pattern remains consistent: a small number of major manufacturers account for the majority of spending, while smaller firms share the remainder. This pattern is especially pronounced when focusing on chronic diseases, which are highly relevant to Medicare coverage, emphasizing the outsized influence of top manufacturers in these therapeutic areas.

Pricing Trends by Disease Condition

Using the dropdown menu, we can look at each of the five chronic conditions individually and compare their total claims and average spending per claim from 2019 to 2023. Looking at the visualization, we can see that for hypertension, the total number of claims is the highest every year and increases slightly from around 253 million claims in 2019 to around 270 million claims in 2023, a growth of roughly 6.7%. However, the average spending per claim rises much more sharply, from around $1,501 in 2019 to about $2,003 in 2023, increasing roughly 33%. This suggests that while the overall use of hypertension medications remained relatively stable with a slight increase, the cost per prescription fill has grown significantly over time.

Summary and additional work

In this visualization analysis, we tried to explore Medicare Drug D prices between 2019-2023 and looked at the overall trends over time. We looked at how costs vary by brand versus generic name and by each manufacturer. We also looked at drug spending by top diseases affecting elderly adults age 65+.

Unfortunately, this data is from 2019-2023, and not reflective of current drug prices. Additionally, there were many rows of data with NaN values, missing price values that were not accounted for so actual prices may be slightly higher than what we have collected.
